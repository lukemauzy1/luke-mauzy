As part of a U.S. Navy affiliated student research project, I developed a Python simulation to optimize a 3-stage missile booster system for maximum delta-V. The study compared two designs: a fully optimized rocket against one constrained to a fixed 10-second first-stage burn — reflecting a real tradeoff the Navy faces with shipboard gas management systems.

## Client Problem

The U.S. Navy seeks efficient missile launch systems that avoid large onboard gas management infrastructure. Popup boosters reduce exhaust complexity but may sacrifice performance. The goal was to quantify exactly how much performance is lost — and whether the tradeoff is worth it.

## Approach

The simulation was built using:

- **Tsiolkovsky rocket equations** to model delta-V across each stage
- **numpy** for numerical modeling of stage lengths and mass ratios
- **scipy.optimize** to find optimal configurations within real physical constraints
- A fixed total rocket length of **7.5 meters**, with structural and bulkhead constraints applied throughout

Two rocket configurations were modeled and compared:

- **Rocket 1** — Equal mass ratios across all three stages (fully optimized)
- **Rocket 2** — Fixed 10-second first-stage burn, with the remaining stages optimized (popup booster simulation)

## Core Simulation

The heart of the simulation is an objective function that computes total delta-V across all three stages using the Tsiolkovsky rocket equation, then passes it to `scipy.optimize.minimize` to find the optimal stage lengths:

```python
def objective(lengths):
    L1, L2, L3 = lengths
    M_s = list(map(structure_mass, [L1, L2, L3]))
    M_f = list(map(fuel_mass, [L1, L2, L3]))

    M0_1 = sum(M_s + M_f) + 3 * bulkhead_mass + payload_mass
    Mf_1 = M0_1 - M_f[0]
    M0_2 = sum(M_s[1:] + M_f[1:]) + 2 * bulkhead_mass + payload_mass
    Mf_2 = M0_2 - M_f[1]
    M0_3 = M_s[2] + M_f[2] + bulkhead_mass + payload_mass
    Mf_3 = M0_3 - M_f[2]

    return -(Ve * (np.log(M0_1 / Mf_1) + np.log(M0_2 / Mf_2) + np.log(M0_3 / Mf_3)))
```

## Results

The simulation produced optimal stage lengths and delta-V distributions for both designs:

- **Rocket 1** distributed performance evenly across all three stages
- **Rocket 2** showed a significantly shorter first stage, with heavier reliance on stages 2 and 3
- The delta-V difference between the two designs was **only 2.08%** — demonstrating that short-duration solid boosters result in only marginal efficiency losses compared to full gas management systems

This finding supports the viability of popup booster designs as a practical simplification for Navy shipboard missile launchers without meaningful performance sacrifice.
