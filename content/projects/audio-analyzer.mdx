For our semester project in Electronics, we designed, built, and tested an analog audio analyzer circuit on a custom PCB. The goal: take a real audio signal, (I used *Dreams* by Fleetwood Mac) split it into high and low frequency bands, and drive two LEDs that respond to the energy in each band in real time.

## System Overview

The circuit takes a stereo audio input via a **3.5mm jack**, sums the channels through an **AC summing amplifier**, then routes the signal through two parallel filter paths. Each path feeds a **peak detector** and ultimately a **LED driver**: one for the lows, one for the highs.

## Circuit Stages

### Input & Power
- Two **3.5mm audio jacks** wired in parallel (only one soldered) for flexibility — a splitter lets you monitor audio on speakers simultaneously
- A **9V battery** feeds a **rail splitter**, creating symmetric ±4.5V rails (treated as ±4.5V / 0V for op-amp biasing)
- A **PMOS polarity protection** transistor disconnects the board if the battery is inserted backwards

### AC Summing Amplifier
- Combines the left and right audio channels into a single mono signal
- AC-coupled to block any DC offset from the source

### Sallen-Key Low-Pass Filter
- Second-order active filter passing the **bass frequencies** of the audio signal
- Cutoff frequency selected to isolate the low-frequency content of the track (chosen as 250Hz)

### Sallen-Key High-Pass Filter
- Second-order active filter passing the **treble frequencies**
- Cutoff frequency tuned to isolate the high frequency content (chosen as 700Hz)

### Peak Detectors
- One for each filter output (LP and HP)
- Captures the **amplitude envelope** of each frequency band
- DC blocking capacitors (C5, C11) and voltage dividers set the signal shift and threshold

### LED Drivers
Two different driver topologies were implemented for educational comparison:

- **Analog LED Driver** — uses a MOSFET (Q2) biased by the peak detector output; the LED brightness scales continuously with signal amplitude
- **PWM LED Driver** — compares the peak detector output against a **triangle-wave oscillator** to generate a PWM signal, switching the LED on/off at a duty cycle proportional to the audio energy

## How It Worked

Playing *Dreams* by Fleetwood Mac through the circuit, the two LEDs visibly responded to the music:

- The **low-frequency LED** pulsed with the kick drum and bass line
- The **high-frequency LED** reacted to the vocals, hi-hats, and guitar

The result was a real-time visual representation of the song's frequency content — exactly what the design intended.
